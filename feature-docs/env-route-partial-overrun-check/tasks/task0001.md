# Task Plan: task0001 — Rescan the in-limit reading on a one-sided env-route overrun

## Goal

When exactly one of the env route's two extglob readings overruns, the bodies and commands decoded by the in-limit reading are rescanned, so a destructive command among them is denied, and the overrun's scan-budget ask is kept for every other outcome.

## Requirements

FR1, FR2, FR3, FR4, FR5, FR6, FR7, NFR1, NFR2, NFR3, NFR4, NFR5

## Scope

### Files to Create

None.

### Files to Modify
- `em-workflow/hooks/destructive-guard.py` — the env-route combine and the queue step, per Design below
- `em-workflow/hooks/tests/destructive-guard-cases.json` — two new whole-hook cases for the one-sided overrun (TS-1, TS-2)
- `tests/test_destructive_guard_env_route.py` — new per-reading unit tests (TS-1 body check, TS-2 held ask, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5) and the expected-value update of two existing tests

## Design

The contracts of the env-route combine, the queue step and the deferred asks, and decisions D1 to D3, are in IMPLEMENTATION.md (Shared Components, Cross-task Design Decisions). This section gives this task's flow and constraints.

### Flow for one env-route statement whose readings returned results

1. Each reading reports its items (candidate bodies and command strings), its overrun and its unrestorable.
2. Count the readings that overran.
   - None overran: existing behaviour.
   - Every returned reading overran (two of two, or the single reading): nothing is queued, the scan-budget ask is held and the `--args-json` steps stop. Existing behaviour.
   - Exactly one of two overran: continue with step 3.
3. Drop every item of the overrun reading, values that individually fit the limit included.
4. From the in-limit reading alone, compute the items and their charge by the existing rule: each string once, an identical item charged once, duplicates inside one reading kept. The overrun reading's items take no part in this comparison, so the read order of the two readings changes nothing.
5. When adding that charge would exceed the scan budget: nothing is queued and the scan-budget ask is held (existing over-budget behaviour).
6. Otherwise: queue the in-limit items for rescan, each bound to its payload word in the same way as the queue step does for an ordinary `--args-json` value; hold the scan-budget ask; stop the `--args-json` steps for the rest of the run.
7. The queued items are rescanned as ordinary commands. When the held asks are settled, a deny from the rescan wins; with no deny, the decision is the scan-budget ask.

### Constraints

- **Overrun stays reported.** In the one-sided case the combine result still reports overrun raised (D3).
- **Unrestorable.** Held as before in every branch, and it does not affect whether anything is queued.
- **Other routes.** The queue step queues under a raised overrun only for an env-route result that carries in-limit items. A plain mcp-bridge run statement and a lifted notion statement from a statically restored command word keep queuing nothing on overrun.
- **Lexer states.** The three lexer states (`_lex_unreadable_met`, `_lex_extglob_met`, `_lex_extglob`) are back at their entry values on every exit path, the new branch included, and no extglob unit is recorded.
- **Existing tests that pin the result shape.** `test_overrun_raised_by_only_one_reading_is_reported` and `test_flags_of_a_statement_that_has_no_value_are_reported` pin the combine's result. Update only their expected values as the new result requires, and keep their assertions that overrun is reported.

### Whole-hook case body (TS-1, TS-2)

Start from the SPEC.md TS-1 description: a line that the extglob-off reading discards as a syntax error, a notion call whose `--args-json` nests deeper than `MAX_ARGS_JSON_DEPTH`, and a separate notion call carrying the command, lifted through a `BASH_FUNC_mcp-bridge` assignment as in the existing env-route lift case. Before relying on it, run the real readings (no substitution) on the same body and confirm that exactly one reading overruns and the other decodes the command. When it does not hold, adjust the body's shape until it does, keeping three properties: one reading overruns, the other decodes the notion command, and the lift goes through the environment assignment.

## Test Notes

- AC-1 and AC-2 are whole-hook cases run by `run-destructive-guard.py`, each with a unit test on the real readings. AC-3, AC-4 and AC-5 are unit tests in `tests/test_destructive_guard_env_route.py`. AC-6 and AC-7 are the regression run and a diff check.
- Write the TS-1 body check (AC-1) first. It decides whether the whole-hook body works before the deny case is pinned.
- The reverse direction (only the extglob-off reading overruns) may not be constructible through the whole hook (REQUIREMENTS.md 10.1); AC-3 covers it through substitution.
- Build the over-budget case (AC-5) with substituted reading results that carry a large charge, not with a genuinely large real scan.
- Do not set `batch` on the new cases: under `CLAUDE_BATCH` an ask becomes a deny and AC-1 could not be told apart from AC-2.
- If, after adjusting the body's shape, no body makes exactly one reading overrun through the whole hook, report it as a plan deviation rather than weakening AC-1.

- Any plugin version change.
- Changes to other hooks, `hooks.json` or other test files.
