# Task Plan: task0001 — A pooled rm deny wins over held non-rm asks, and cases pin the reason id

## Goal

When a compound command holds a recursive delete that the hook denies on its own, destructive-guard emits `deny` with the recursive-delete reason id (rm-recursive / rm-root) and reason text. This holds whatever the segment order, the separator or the other checks in the command. The case runner can also pin a case's reason id, and the case table gains rule-pinned cases that detect the regression in both segment orders.

## Design

The case-format contract, the verdict precedence, and the feature-wide conventions (case placement, generic paths, existing pins, test environment, no version change) are in IMPLEMENTATION.md. This plan references them and does not restate them.

### Vocabulary

- **Statement iteration**: one pass of main()'s loop over one statement that statements() yields. This is SPEC.md's 「文」. Two iterations are different even when their text is equal.
- **Non-rm ask source**: an `ask` that a check other than the rm judgment reaches inside the loop. The sources are:
  - self-modification;
  - git-amend, on the plain git route and on the substitution-headed git route;
  - archive-overwrite;
  - the in-loop gh-pr-merge;
  - systemctl-system, cloud-iam, kubectl-destructive, terraform, cloud-delete and remote-exec;
  - chmod-broad and chown-recursive.
- **Held ask**: a recorded non-rm `ask`: its tier, reason id and reason text as authored, before demotion. Held asks are kept in arrival order.
- **Early stop**: the scan ends before statements() is exhausted. There are two kinds:
  - the scan-budget overrun that statements() detects, either when it takes a chunk or after a chunk's statements (the unclosed-form check);
  - a lexer budget overrun or an unmatched subscript raised out of the iteration.
- **rm candidates**: the pooled rm records, minus the records of the statement iterations that Part 2 excludes.

### Part 1 — Collection during the loop (FR1, FR2)

1. Every `deny` path stays immediate: curl-pipe-shell before the loop; and inside the loop safety-bypass, transcript-write, every git, file-destruction and external `deny`, and the git route of a substitution-headed statement (A2, A8).
2. A non-rm `ask` is recorded as a held ask instead of being emitted. The scan goes on: the remaining checks of the same statement run, and so do all later statements.
   - Whether the check that produced the `ask` keeps evaluating its own remaining conditions is the implementer's choice.
   - Either way, a `deny` reached afterwards is emitted at once. This is a permitted strengthening (NFR3), and the first held ask keeps its place.
3. rm records are pooled as today (D6). Each record also notes the statement iteration it came from.
4. The collection state belongs to one judgment. run() may judge one payload twice, once per lexer reading, and each judgment starts with empty state.
5. Nothing is demoted while collecting.

### Part 2 — The FR8 exclusion

A statement iteration is excluded when both of these hold:

- (a) the self-modification `ask` was reached in this iteration;
- (b) a deletion operand of the rm invocation established in this iteration matches the protected self-config pattern, in its raw or in its normalized form (the same matching the self-modification check applies). The operands are the rm's ARGS on the plain route, and the REMAINDER on the substitution-headed route.

When an iteration is excluded, every rm record of that iteration leaves the candidates: rm-recursive, rm-root and rm-unresolvable, from either route.

Rules:
- A match only in a redirect target (A7) excludes nothing. Neither does another write source, or a protected path in a different iteration (another statement, a substitution body, a `-c` / eval payload, a heredoc body).
- On the parse-failure fallback (lexed false), condition (b) uses the same operand test. The fallback's whole-segment match is not enough on its own.
- The self-modification `ask` itself is held like any other non-rm `ask`.
- The excluded rm invocation keeps its ordinal. The designations of the other rm invocations still count it: in TS-13 the second rm is `2番目のrmの1番目の対象`.

### Part 3 — Early stop (FR4)

1. A scan-budget overrun inside statements() no longer emits a verdict itself. It ends the scan and gives main() the scan-budget `ask` as the early-stop ask.
2. main() catches a lexer budget overrun or an unmatched subscript raised while the loop iterates. The early-stop ask is the unmatched-subscript `ask` for an unmatched subscript, and the scan-budget `ask` otherwise. This is the same choice the existing unsettled-lex handling makes.
3. After an early stop, no further statement is scanned.
4. The handler in the per-reading judgment stays as the fallback for anything raised outside the loop.
5. statements() keeps:
   - the shape of what it yields;
   - its unmatched-subscript behaviour with and without the unsettled list.

   Tests under `tests/` call statements(), the per-reading judgment and run() directly.

### Part 4 — Resolution (FR3, NFR2)

One resolution step ends every judgment: normal end, early stop, unreadable chunks, or the deferral to the sibling guards. It applies these rules in order:

1. The strongest rm candidate is a `deny`: emit it. Its reason id is rm-root when any winner is rm-root, otherwise rm-recursive. Its reason is the existing rm rendering and selection, unchanged.
2. Otherwise, emit the first held ask.
3. Otherwise, after an early stop, emit the early-stop ask.
4. Otherwise, after a normal end with an unreadable chunk, emit that chunk's `ask` (existing).
5. Otherwise, emit the PR-merge fallback `ask` when its existing condition holds (normal end only).
6. Otherwise, emit the strongest rm candidate when it is an `ask` (rm-unresolvable).
7. Otherwise, apply the existing final allow / silence logic.

Every emission goes through the existing single emission routine. That routine alone applies the CLAUDE_BATCH demotion, after the choice above (FR3).

Postconditions:
- When any rm candidate is a `deny`, the output is `deny` with the rm reason id. The reason is the one the rm records render to, so it contains no text of any non-rm check and no demotion sentence.
- When no rm candidate is a `deny` and no non-rm `deny` was reached, the decision and the reason id are the ones the unmodified hook gives.
- No judgment ends in `allow` or silence while a held ask, an early-stop ask or an rm `ask` exists.
- The post-processing in run() is untouched: the extglob-switch `ask` and the unreadable heredoc delimiter `ask`.

### Part 5 — Runner (FR5)

The runner implements the case-format contract in IMPLEMENTATION.md Shared Components.

- **Reading the reason id.** When permissionDecisionReason starts with `[destructive-guard/`, the reason id is the text from there up to the first `]`. Otherwise there is none.
- **FAIL line.** A failing case prints the obtained reason id in addition to what it prints today. The timeout marker stays.
- **Unchanged.** The final unattended-demotion check, the path-argument mode, the `{merge_fixture}` handling and the exit status rule.
