### Reserved words after closers and name-taking keywords (P10; finding 42180fe3cd060309)

- The lexer's grammar state gains a "reserved word acceptable" state that is not itself a command position. It is entered directly after a closer: the `)` that closes a subshell group, the `}` that closes a brace group, the `))` that closes an arithmetic-command region, the `]]` that closes a conditional command opened at a command position, and the reserved words `fi`, `done` and `esac`.
- In that state, a word that is a reserved word is recognized as one, and a recognized reserved word sets (or does not set) command position exactly as P3 states for it. So `then` / `do` / `else` / `elif` read after a closer put the next `((` at a command position.
- `do` directly after `for NAME` or `select NAME` is a reserved word and sets command position.
- The position directly after `coproc NAME`, after `function NAME` and after `function NAME ()` is a command position.
- Unchanged: a word at an argument position (after a command word, or after a substitution's closing `)`) is never a reserved word; `[[` / `]]` that are arguments are not a conditional command.

### Heredoc body start (P11; finding 6ad3de37b64392ec)

- When a real heredoc operator is registered, the lexer records which regions were open at that moment. The pending body begins on the line after the first newline read later while no region opened after the operator is still open.
- A double quote, single quote, ANSI-C quote, `$(`, backtick or expansion that opens after the operator and runs over a newline defers the body: the body starts after the newline ending the line on which that region closes, under the same rule.
- A backslash-newline is a line continuation and never starts a body.
- A region enclosing the operator (a command or process substitution the operator sits inside) defers nothing, whether it is still open at that newline or closed before it; this is the pre-rework handling for that shape and stays unchanged.
- Several operators pending at the same newline keep their bodies in operator order, as today. strip_heredocs() and the position map read the body line ranges the lexer reports; their contracts do not change.

### Word after a process substitution (P12; finding acf9e8aa8bb287ea)

- Closing a `<(...)` / `>(...)` leaves the enclosing word in progress, exactly as closing a `$(...)` does. A `#` directly after the closing `)` is word text, not a comment; a `#` after whitespace that follows it still starts a comment (P5).
- D5's process-substitution evidence (has_process_sub) is unchanged.
