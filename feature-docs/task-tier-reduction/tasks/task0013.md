Give the tier decision one recorded shape with a single owned mapping into
its two projections, one basis vocabulary across all three, a correct
persistence path, and a procedure text whose two-reading rule cites the
evaluator that enforces it instead of standing alone as prose.

## Design

**The defect.** The decision exists in three shapes — the persisted record,
the workflow file's decision block, and the retrospect signal — whose
members correspond neither by name nor by granularity, and no document owns
the correspondence. The transcription instruction therefore tells the
orchestrator to read one shape and write another without saying how. On top
of that, the basis identifier is spelled one way in the rules file and the
evaluator and another way in the phase-state schema and the procedure, and
the workflow schema names the persisted record at a path whose segments are
in the wrong order.

**One owner for the mapping.** The transcription section of the create-spec
phase protocol becomes the single owner of a mapping table from the
persisted record's fields to the decision block's four sub-fields and to
the retrospect signal's members. The other two documents cite that table by
path instead of restating it. This is the same SSOT discipline the feature
already applies to the reason codes and the reduction table.

**The decision block keeps its four sub-fields.** No sub-field is added or
removed — the specification fixes the set. What the mapping settles is
where each one comes from: the deciding agent, the observed probability
values and the timestamp are projections of the persisted record, and the
list of subtractions is a projection of the tier through the develop
skill's reduction table. Stating the last one as a projection is what
removes the duplicate-table objection without changing the schema: the
table is read, never copied.

**One basis vocabulary.** The rules file's decision-basis values are the
only source. The phase-state schema's example values, the procedure's two
readings and the retrospect signal all use those values; the divergent
spelling disappears. The rules file itself is task0012's file — this task
consumes the vocabulary and does not redefine it.

**The two-reading rule gets its enforcing interface.** The procedure states
how many times the judgement skill is invoked, which two bases those
invocations correspond to, what is compared between the two readings, and
that a disagreement resolves to the tier that removes nothing — and it
attributes that resolution to the evaluator's input contract
(IMPLEMENTATION.md Shared Components), which task0012 extends to take both
readings. The rules file's note on the same rule becomes a citation of that
contract rather than a second statement of it.

## Test Notes

AC-3 and AC-4 are repository-wide absence checks — the divergent basis
spelling and the wrongly ordered path must be absent from the whole plugin
tree, not merely corrected in one file — which is what makes them fail on
the pre-change tree and stay meaningful afterwards. Both are cheap text
scans over the plugin directory.

AC-1's single-ownership half is asserted by requiring the mapping's rows in
exactly one document and requiring the other two to carry a citation of its
path; the negative proof is a forged sample in which a second document
restates a row.

`tests/test_tier_workflow_schema.py` may hold assertions pinned to the
pre-change schema text; any such assertion is updated inside this task.
