# Task Plan: task0001 — Rescue the provisional chain at `)` when its chain base is a subshell

## Goal

Both copies of codex-hook-interactive-guard read an unquoted `)` that ends a
chain of provisional groups inside a subshell as the close of that subshell,
after rescuing the chain, so the reproduction
`zsh -c 'setopt nobadpattern; ( function { :; } foo(a; python3 -i; )'` and its
variants are denied. Every `)` that does not meet the condition keeps its present
reading.

### Where the change sits

Only the reading of an unquoted `)` that the main reader itself meets changes
(the parser's close-paren handling, `_close_paren`). A `)` inside quotes, inside
a comment, inside a substitution chunk, or taken into an argument range by the
range scan never reaches it. The range scan, `_mark_provisional`, the `}`
rescue, the end-of-input rescue, `_rescue_separator` and everything after
parsing are unchanged.

### Rescue condition at `)`

Evaluated after the word in progress is finished (the present first step of the
`)` handling — a lone `}` finished there keeps its own reading first), against
G, the innermost open group at that moment. The rescue applies exactly when all
three hold:

1. G is provisional and its `outer_blocked` is false.
2. `_rescue_separator` accepts G's own first direct separator: it is a `;` or a
   lone `&`. G is the innermost group of its chain, so it has nothing to
   inherit; the outer groups' separators are already folded into
   `outer_blocked`. Only the separators the reader read count, never the range
   lookahead.
3. G's `chain_base` is a `(` group. None (the top level) and a `{` group do not
   qualify (FR4). A chain base is never provisional, by its definition.

Unlike the `}` rule there is no word condition: words pending before the `)`
never block the rescue (FR5).

Contract: constant time; reads only G, G's chain base and the values
`_mark_provisional` fixed when G opened; changes no state. When it does not
hold, the `)` is read exactly as today: it closes the innermost group if that is
a `(` group, and is otherwise a separator.

The condition may be a sibling of the `}` predicate (`_closes_after_rescue`) or
an extension of it that takes the required chain-base kind and leaves out the
word rule for `)`. Either way the separator test stays the one
`_rescue_separator` definition (FR3).

### Rescue action at `)`

When the condition holds:

1. Remove the open groups above the chain base from the stack of open groups,
   innermost first. Each provisional group of the chain is removed once and is
   never closed: its `(` reads as an argument-word character and the group ends
   at its first direct separator.
2. Finalize the pending words, if any, as one simple command of the chain base
   (FR5), the way the present `)` finalizes the words before it. No separator is
   recorded on any group: the chain base is not provisional, and the `)` is no
   separator.
3. Close the chain base exactly as the present `)` closes a `(` group: it
   becomes the group just closed, so a stdin redirect read next connects it.
4. The `)` is consumed.

Postconditions (FR6):

- Commands read in the rescued groups keep their group. Their connection and
  skip are settled after reading from the groups they sit in, so a pipe into the
  chain base, a stdin redirect after its close and a function-definition body
  around it reach them (TS-8, TS-9, TS-10).
- Text after the close is read outside the chain base (TS-4).
- A later `)` with no open `(` group is a separator, by the existing branch
  (TS-13).

### Readings that stay as they are (FR4)

- Chain base None: row 560, `function { :; } foo(a; python3 -i)`, still closes
  the provisional group at `)` and is denied.
- Chain base a `{` group: the `)` closes the provisional group as today; the `}`
  rescue rules are unchanged.
- An innermost group that is not provisional, a blocked chain, or a first direct
  separator other than `;` and a lone `&` (TS-6, TS-7): as today.
- A quoted `)` (TS-14) and a `)` in a comment (TS-11) never reach the reader.

### Cost (NFR1, NFR2)

The check costs constant time. Over the whole parse every group is pushed once
and removed at most once, so the rescue adds amortized constant work per group.
No function calls itself and no new call cycle forms.

### Documentation (FR9)

Update these docstrings so they describe the `)` rescue:

- Module docstring, step 4: a provisional group is rescued in three cases —
  input ends with it open, a lone `}` with a `{` chain base, and a `)` with a
  `(` chain base. State the `)` case: its condition, that pending words become a
  command of the closed group, that the commands of the rescued groups are
  judged as commands of that group, that text after the close is outside it,
  and that a later unmatched `)` is a separator. Generalize the wording that
  counts two cases.
- `_Parser`: the rescue is made in three places, not two.
- `_Group`: `provisional` (the three occasions), `chain_base` (a `}` rescues
  only with a `{` chain base, a `)` only with a `(` chain base),
  `outer_blocked` (read at a `}` or a `)` in constant time).
- `_rescue_separator`: applied by the three rescues.
- `_rescue_argument_groups`: a provisional group that a `}` or a `)` rescued is
  no longer open when input ends.
- `_read_argument_parens`: the provisional group may also be rescued at a `)`
  that closes the `(` group around its chain.
- `_close_paren`, and every method this task adds or changes.

Constraints: every marker sentence the documentation tests check stays
(DOCSTRING_MARKERS, RESCUE_PROVISIONAL_MARKER, RESCUE_MARKERS,
BRACE_CLOSE_MARKER, COMMENT_EXCEPTION_MARKERS, ANONYMOUS_ARGUMENT_MARKERS, the
group marker sets, and the backquote and delimiter markers); no sentence of
STALE_DOCSTRING_SENTENCES or STALE_GROUP_SENTENCES is written back. Add one
marker sentence for the `)` rescue (its wording is the implementer's) and a
documentation test that finds it in the module docstring and in the `_Parser`
docstring of both copies.
