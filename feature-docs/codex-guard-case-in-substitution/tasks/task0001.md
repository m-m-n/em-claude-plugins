### Reading model of a case statement

When the keyword is recognized, the skip no longer raises; it moves past the
four letters and opens a case statement as a new entry on its explicit stack,
above the context that held the keyword. The statement passes through the
phases below. "Blank" means a space or a tab. "Read by the current rules"
means a backslash pair, a single-quoted unit, `$'...'`, a double-quoted unit,
`$(`, `$((`, `${` and a backquote span are followed exactly as in the `p`
context today, each opening its own stack entry where it does now.

**Word-start rule (applies to phases 1, 4, 5 and 7).** A phase that waits for
the start of a word (subject, pattern, body) decides the phase change on the
first character of the word before that character is read. A word that
begins with a backslash pair, a quote, `$'`, `$(`, `${` or a backquote
therefore begins the subject, pattern or body like any other character
(TS8's `')'` pattern depends on this).

1. **Header, before the subject.** Blanks and newlines are skipped; a comment
   is skipped (see Comments). An unquoted `;`, `&`, `|` or `)` here leaves the
   statement with no subject and no `in`: undecidable (FR4 (1)). Any other
   character begins the subject (phase 2) and is read there, so a `(` opens a
   subject group and `<(` or `>(` a process substitution (D3).
2. **Header, subject.** Read by the current rules. `<(` and `>(` open a
   command-list context, like `$(`. A `(` opens a parenthesised group of the
   subject (one stack entry per open parenthesis) and its matching `)` closes
   it; inside a group every other character belongs to the subject, blanks,
   `|`, `#`, `;`, `&`, `<`, `>` and newlines included (D3). Outside groups: a
   blank or newline ends the subject (phase 3); an unquoted `;`, `&`, `|` or
   `)` ends it too, and since what follows is not `in`, the statement is
   undecidable (FR4 (1)); `<`, `>`, `#` and every other character are subject
   characters (zsh numeric ranges such as `<1-2>` included).
3. **Header, after the subject.** Blanks and newlines are skipped; a comment
   is skipped. The next characters must be `in` followed by a blank, a
   newline, the end of the input, or one of `;`, `&`, `|`, `(`, `)`, `<`,
   `>`; the statement is then at a pattern position (phase 4), and the
   character after `in` is read under phase 4's rules. Anything else is
   undecidable, zsh's brace form included (FR4 (1)).
4. **Pattern position** (right after `in`, right after an item terminator).
   Blanks and newlines are skipped; a comment is skipped. A standalone `esac`
   closes the statement (step 9). A `)` is undecidable (FR4 (3)). A `(` is
   the optional leading parenthesis (phase 5). An unquoted `;` or `&` is
   undecidable (D3). Any other character, `|` (zsh's empty leading
   alternative), `<` and `>` included, begins a pattern (phase 6) and is read
   there. Every word other than `esac`, `case` included, is a pattern here
   (D1).
5. **After the optional leading `(`.** Blanks and newlines are skipped; a
   comment is skipped (SPEC.md A5). A `)` is undecidable (no pattern,
   FR4 (3)). An unquoted `;` or `&` is undecidable (D3). Any other character,
   `|` included, begins the pattern (phase 6); a `(` there opens a nested
   group.
6. **Pattern.** Read by the current rules, so a `)` or `|` inside a quoted
   unit or a substitution closes nothing (FR2). A `|` separates alternatives,
   empty ones included; blanks are skipped. A `(` opens a nested group (one
   stack entry per open parenthesis). A `)` closes the innermost open nested
   group, or, when none is open, closes the pattern and starts the body
   (phase 7). Inside a nested group every other character belongs to the
   pattern, blanks, `|`, `#`, `;`, `&`, `<`, `>` and newlines included (D3).
   Outside nested groups: an unquoted newline, `;` or `&` is undecidable
   (D3); a comment is skipped; `<`, `>` and every other character are pattern
   characters.
7. **Body start.** Blanks and newlines are skipped. A standalone `case`
   (followed by a blank or newline, as `_is_case_keyword` requires) is at a
   command position: the body moves to phase 8 and a nested case statement
   opens above it. A standalone `esac` closes the statement (empty body). Any
   other character moves the body to phase 8 and is read there.
8. **Body.** Read as a command list by the rules of the `p` context
   (substitutions, comments, `<<<`, heredoc undecidable), with these
   differences:
   - the item terminators `;;&`, `;;`, `;&` and `;|`, matched longest first,
     end the item and return the statement to a pattern position (phase 4);
     a lone `;` is an ordinary separator;
   - a `(` opens a plain command-list context (a subshell) above the body;
     the `)` that closes it returns to the body;
   - a `)` read while the body itself is the innermost context is
     undecidable (D2);
   - a standalone `esac` at a command position closes the statement (step 9);
   - a `case` at a command position opens a nested case statement above the
     body.
   Command positions in a body are the ones `_is_case_keyword` recognizes
   today (after blanks, one of `;` `&` `|` `(` newline `{` `!`, or one of its
   reserved words), applied to `esac` the same way as to `case`. So an
   `esac` that is an argument (`echo esac`) or sits in a comment closes
   nothing (TS10).
9. **Closing.** A closing `esac` moves the reader past its four letters and
   removes the statement's entries from the stack; reading resumes in the
   context that held the `case` keyword. A `)` there closes the substitution
   (or the enclosing group) as it does today (FR1). Input that ends while any
   statement is open is undecidable through the existing end-of-input rule
   (FR4 (2)).

**Standalone `esac`**: the four letters followed by the end of the input, a
blank, a newline, `;`, `&`, `|`, `)`, `<` or `>`. At a pattern position the
reader is at a word start by construction; in a body the command-position
rule above supplies the left side.

**Comments.** In phases 1, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8, and in phase 6 outside nested
groups, an unquoted `#` that the comment rule of the `p` context would read
as a comment start (the character before it is a blank, a newline, `;`, `&`,
`|` or `(`) starts a comment that runs to the end of the line (SPEC.md A5 for
the pattern positions); input that ends inside it is undecidable as today. In
the subject and inside any parenthesised group of the subject or a pattern,
`#` is an ordinary character. In a pattern outside its groups, the comment is
followed by phase 6's newline rule.

**Nesting.** A `case` statement inside a body, a substitution or process
substitution inside the subject, a substitution inside a pattern or a body, a
parenthesised group inside the subject or a pattern, and a subshell inside a
body each add an entry to the one explicit stack. The substitution can end
only when every entry above its own has been removed, so a pattern's `)`
closes neither the substitution nor a group opened inside it (FR1).

**Helpers.** Any helper the skip gains is a bounded lookup (keyword at a
command position, standalone word, `in` check). No helper calls
`_skip_substitution`, `_read_quoted_unit`, `parse` or `_remove_range_quotes`,
and no helper calls itself (NFR2). If `_is_case_keyword` is generalized (for
example to judge `esac` as well), its judgment of `case` in the `p` context
must stay exactly as today (A3).

**Cost.** One pass: each character is consumed once by the main loop; a phase
change is constant work; forward checks look at most four characters ahead;
the backward look for a keyword covers only the preceding blank run and the
letter run before it (NFR1).

### Undecidable reasons

The reason texts are internal and not part of any contract; the skip no

### Docstrings (FR9, D4)

- `_skip_substitution`: replace "a heredoc or a `case` inside it" with
  statements that a `case` statement inside the chunk is read (its pattern's
  `)` closes neither the chunk nor a group), and that each form below makes
  the chunk undecidable: no `in` after the subject (zsh's brace form
  included), no closing `esac` before the input ends, a `)` with no pattern
  at a pattern position, a heredoc anywhere in the chunk (item bodies
  included), an unmatched `)` in an item body, an unquoted `;` or `&` where a
  pattern must begin, and an unquoted newline, `;` or `&` in a pattern outside
  its groups. List the new stack contexts next to the existing ones.
- `_is_case_keyword`: when its behaviour or signature changes, its docstring
  states the new rule and that the `case` judgment in a command list is
  unchanged.
- Module docstring: unchanged (D4).
