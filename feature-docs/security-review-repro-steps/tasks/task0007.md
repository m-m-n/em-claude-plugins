# Task Plan: task0007 — Unverifiable security findings stay open instead of declined (both review phases)

## Goal

On the orchestrator verification paths of both review phases, a security
finding whose steps are `unverifiable` and whose basis the orchestrator does
not confirm by its own reading is no longer recorded as `declined`. It is
recorded as `unresolved` with a `resolution_reason` beginning `unverified`,
stays out of auto-fix, the residual critical / high count and send-back as
FR6 requires, is not dropped by round-context suppression, and is verified
again in the next round.

## Design

Contracts used: IMPLEMENTATION.md D7 (added by this rework; it revises the
`unverifiable` cell of D2's two orchestrator rows and the `unverified` part of
the "`not reproduced` and `unverified` literals" row), together with the
unchanged rows "Verification outcome vocabulary", "Verifier constraints" and
D3 / D4 / D6. SPEC.md is not changed: FR6's "not a fix / send-back target
unless the basis is confirmed" is kept exactly; only the record of such a
finding moves from `declined` to `unresolved`.

### Outcome mapping on orchestrator paths (both documents)

Unchanged:

- `reproduced` → stays a target.
- `not reproduced` → `resolution: declined`, `resolution_reason` beginning
  `not reproduced` (carried by round-context suppression, FR13).
- `unverifiable` with the basis confirmed by the orchestrator's own reading →
  stays a target.
- No steps → orchestrator judgment; judged not to address → `declined` with
  that judgment as the reason.
- Truncated or over-4096-byte values are `unverifiable`; the merged-finding
  rule of D6 is unchanged.

Changed:

- `unverifiable` with the basis not confirmed → `resolution: unresolved`,
  `resolution_reason` beginning `unverified`. Never `declined`.

### The shared sentences (identical in both documents)

Both verification sections gain these two sentences, word for word:

> An `unverifiable` finding whose basis the orchestrator does not confirm by
> its own reading is never `declined`: it is recorded with
> `resolution: unresolved` and a `resolution_reason` beginning `unverified`.
> Such a finding is neither an auto-fix candidate nor counted in the residual
> critical/high count, so it is never sent back for a fix on that ground;
> round-context suppression does not drop it, because it is not `declined`,
> and the next round verifies it again.

The resolution vocabulary (`fixed` / `declined` / `deferred` / `unresolved`)
does not change. Round-context suppression keeps dropping only `declined`
entries, so the existing round_context build carries the finding with
`resolution: unresolved` and reviewers keep treating it as an open item.
