# Task Plan: task0001 — Judge zsh anonymous-function bodies, their arguments and their pipe connection

## Goal

Both copies of codex-hook-interactive-guard judge the body of the three zsh
anonymous-function forms (`function { ...; }`, `function () { ...; } arg`,
`() { ...; } arg`), read the words after the body's closing brace as the
function's arguments, and pass a pipe connection on the anonymous-function
head to its body. Regression cases, a linearity input and the documentation
follow the new rules.

## Design

### Terms

- **Reserved-run removal**: the leading-run removal `_names_function` applies
  today: the words of `RESERVED_WORDS` from the start, a non-dynamic `-p`
  right after a removed `time`, a dynamic word never removed, a reserved word
  later in the words never removed, and the original words judged when
  nothing remains.
- **Bare word**: a word holding no quote, escape or expansion (the parser's
  existing bare flag).
- **Anonymous-function head**, one of:
  - **H1 (parenless, FR1)**: within one simple command on one line, the words
    read so far are, after the reserved-run removal, exactly one bare
    `function`, and the next word is a standalone bare `{`.
  - **H2 (FR2 b)**: the words before a `(` are, after the reserved-run
    removal, exactly one bare `function`, and that `(` group closes empty.
    This is the reading `_names_function` already rejects as a definition
    name.
  - **H3 (FR2 c)**: a `(` read while the simple command in progress holds no
    word at all, and that group closes empty. A reserved word before `()`
    (`then ()`) is not H3; it keeps the existing function-name judgment (A5).
- **Anonymous body**: the brace group opened by H1's `{`; or, for H2 and H3,
  the group that follows the empty `()` when it is a brace group. Newlines
  may come between the `()` and the `{`, the same way a definition body may
  follow `name()` across newlines today (A4).

### Behavior contract

1. **Body judged (FR1, FR2)**: the commands inside an anonymous body are
   judged like those of any other group. A body nested in a function
   definition's body stays skipped through the existing settle rule.
2. **Arguments (FR2)**: after an anonymous body's closing `}`, every word up
   to the end of the simple command is an argument of the anonymous function.
   The end is the next `;` `&&` `||` `|` `|&` `&` or newline, or the close of
   an enclosing subshell. An argument forms no judged command and does not
   make the string undecidable. Redirections among or after the arguments
   keep their meaning: a stdin redirect (`<` `<<` `<<<` `<&` `0<`) connects
   the body exactly as when written right after the `}`; other redirects do
   not connect it.
3. **Pipe connection (FR3)**: when the head is the right side of `|` or `|&`
   (the simple command holding H1's or H2's `function`, or H3's `(`), the body
   group is marked as the right side of the pipe. The existing settle pass then
   connects every command and nested group inside it. Nothing after the
   argument span receives the connection. An explicit interactive launch
   (`-i` and the like) is denied under the connection, through the existing
   judge order.
4. **Unchanged readings (FR4)**: words after a brace group that is not an
   anonymous body, and after a function definition's body, still make the
   string undecidable. `function name {`, a quoted or escaped `function`, and
   `function` followed by a newline and then `{` are not H1: the `{` is read
   as it is today. `'function' ()` and `function function ()` stay
   definitions. The judgment of `function () { ...; }` without arguments is
   unchanged.
5. **Subshell after the empty `()`**: when H2's or H3's `()` is followed by a
   subshell instead of a brace group, the existing reading applies; that
   subshell is not an anonymous body.

### Complexity and structure constraints (NFR2, NFR3)

- H1 is decided in constant time at each `{`, from per-command state that is
  updated as each word is appended: whether the words so far still form a
  reserved run, and whether the last word appended is a bare `function`
  directly after that run. The word list is never re-read at a `{`, so a
  command with many `{` words stays linear in its length.
- `_names_function` keeps its name and stays one forward pass that does not
  modify the word list. H2 may reuse its removal or the H1 state.
- Reading stays one forward pass over the string, and group settling stays
  the single post-pass. No new recursion, no cycle, standard library only.

### Documentation (FR5)

- **Module docstring, step 4**: replace the sentence saying `{` and `}`
  delimit a group only as a standalone word at command position with the new
  rule: a `{` also opens a group right after an H1 head. Add the three
  anonymous-function forms, the argument reading after the body's `}`, the
  stdin redirect after the arguments, and the pipe connection passing to the
  body and no further.
- **`_Group` docstring**: describe how a group is marked as an anonymous body
  and where its pipe connection comes from.
- **Docstring of the method that decides H1**: state the H1 rule, that it uses
  the same reserved-run removal, and that the decision takes constant time per
  `{`.
- Every sentence the existing documentation tests pin stays word for word.

  `)`: the existing reading stays.
- A `}` that is not at command position (zsh closes a brace group there; the
  guard does not, as today).
- `function` and `{` on different lines (A4).
- Distinguishing zsh from other shells (A6).
- The scan and judge layers, the deny output and its reason text,
  `.claude/rules/hook-tests.md`, and plugin version files.
