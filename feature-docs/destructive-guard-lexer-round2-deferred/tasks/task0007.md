# Task Plan: task0007 — Read past line continuations when recognizing `$` openers and when splitting words

## Goal

Bash 5.3 removes a backslash-newline pair before it reads the characters
around it. The lexer recognizes an expansion opener whose characters a line
continuation separates (`$\<newline>{ cmd; }` is the command form), and the
word split the decision layer reads takes every such pair out, so
`r\<newline>m` is the word `rm` and a continuation before a command word
does not hide it (review round 1; findings under Provenance).

## Requirements

FR7, FR9, FR10, FR11, NFR1, NFR4, NFR5, NFR6, NFR7

## Provenance

### Opener recognition in the `$` handling

- Wherever the `$` handling runs (shell-rule frames, double quotes,
  expansions, subscripts, the literal top level of an unquoted
  here-document body), the character after `$`, and the second `(` of
  `$((`, are found after skipping any run of backslash-newline pairs, as
  the existing look-ahead after `${` already does. The openers concerned are
  `${` (both forms), `$(`, `$((`, `$[`, `$'` and `$"`.
- The recognized opener is then decided exactly as today for its kind: the
  command form of `${` before any settle or tail decision, every other kind
  through the settle channel and the tail gate unchanged.
- The region starts at the `$`. The look-ahead never passes the current
  reading's bound (the end of the text, a discarded line, or a backtick
  extent, task0006).
- Downstream, the inner text of a command form starts after its opener and
  every continuation pair inside or after it (and after the `|` of `${|`),
  in the structure scan's span inner text, in the unclosed-form inner text
  and in here-document body extraction alike.

### The word split

- The text the tokenizer splits (in its layout mode and its plain-word mode)
  has every backslash-newline pair removed that bash removes: every pair
  outside single-quoted text. A pair inside single quotes stays as it is;
  a backslash before any other character, a `\r` included, is untouched.
- The offsets the tokenizer reports stay offsets in the original text: a
  word that held a pair spans the pair as well.
- Lines of a here-document body are cut before the split as today and are
  not affected.
