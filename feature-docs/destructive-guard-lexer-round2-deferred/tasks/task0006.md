# Task Plan: task0006 — Fix a backtick substitution's extent at the next unescaped backtick before reading its content

## Goal

A backtick substitution ends at the next unescaped backtick, whatever its
content holds, as bash 5.3 decides before it reads that content as a script.
No comment, quote, expansion, subscript or command form opened inside it
can carry the reading past that backtick, so the commands after it, and the
content itself, are inspected (review round 1; findings under
Provenance).

## Requirements

FR7, FR9, FR10, FR11, NFR1, NFR4, NFR5, NFR6, NFR7

## Provenance

- source: review
- review_round: 1
- source_ids: e79128f331828941, e6478d6af2a50e57, 31165687b890b8bc, d1d035263eb03a8a

All four share one root cause: the lexer finds the end of a backtick
substitution only as it reads the content, so an inner context swallows the
closing backtick (e79128f331828941, pre-existing for plain backticks), and
the partial repair for command forms opened inside a backtick
(the bt-form unwinding) misses frame kinds (e6478d6af2a50e57), treats an
escaped backtick as the end (31165687b890b8bc) and loses the backtick
context through quotes and nested substitutions (d1d035263eb03a8a).

### Extent

- When a backtick opens a substitution (at any level where the lexer opens
  one today: shell-rule frames, double quotes, parameter expansions,
  arithmetic, bracket arithmetic, subscripts, extglob, command forms, the
  literal top level of an unquoted here-document body), its extent ends at
  the next backtick that is not escaped. A backtick is escaped when it is
  the second character of a backslash pair read from the opening backtick
  on (equivalently: it is preceded by an odd-length run of backslashes).
  Quotes, comments and expansions inside the extent play no part in finding
  it. With no such backtick, the extent ends where the current reading ends
  today for an unclosed backtick substitution.
- The content is read up to the extent only. Every frame and region opened
  inside it that is still open there ends unclosed at the extent (region end
  at the backtick, not closed), and the backtick then closes the
  substitution, which stays a closed span.
- A here-document whose operator lies inside the extent takes its body only
  from lines inside the extent; a line after the closing backtick is never a
  body line of it (the existing case labelled `heredoc-syntax-error E-7`
  keeps its reading and verdict).
- This rule replaces the command-form-specific handling (the bt-form check
  and unwinding, the backtick searches inside the comment, single-quote and
  ANSI-C branches, and the backtick flag on command-form frames). Any frame
  or pass-state field this adds or removes is classified in the resume
  snapshot.
- Finding the extents costs work linear in the text length overall (for
  example one index of unescaped backticks per lexed text), never a rescan
  per backtick.

## Out of Scope

- Unescaping the content of a backtick substitution (`\``, `\\`, `\$`) for
  any purpose other than finding its extent.
- Nested backtick substitutions written with escaped backticks.
- Any change to `$(` / `${ ` reading outside a backtick extent.
