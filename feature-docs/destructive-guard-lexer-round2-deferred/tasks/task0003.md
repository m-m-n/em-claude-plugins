# Task Plan: task0003 — End delimiter words and lines at `\n` only, and decide unquoted close lines on continuation-joined lines

## Goal

A here-document delimiter word ends only at a metacharacter, and the line
index splits lines only at `\n`, as bash 5.3 does, so `\r`, `\x0b`, `\x0c`,
`\x1c`-`\x1e`, `\x85`, U+2028 and U+2029 no longer shift a body over the
lines bash runs (finding e959ba60bde865a4). For an unquoted delimiter, the
close line is decided on lines joined at backslash-newline, as bash reads
them, so a body no longer closes early and leaves a command substitution
behind a `#` (finding 2b52be5874de85f4).

### FR3: `\n` is the only line separator

- Line starts of the line index are offset 0 and every offset right after a
  `\n`. No other character starts a line, for body starts, close lines, the
  pending-operator line trigger, or any other reader of the index.
- The delimiter word ends at a metacharacter only: space, tab, `\n`, `;`,
  `|`, `&`, `(`, `)`, `<`, `>`. Every other character listed in SPEC.md FR3
  is a character of the word, unquoted or inside quotes, and of the
  delimiter value.
- "On the operator's line" in the delimiter reader (a quote must close
  there; a backslash before a line break makes the word unreadable) means
  before the next `\n`. A backslash before one of the other characters
  escapes it like any ordinary character.
- The blank set of the shell-rule frames (`\r` there) is task0004's; this
  task leaves it unchanged, so on this branch a `\r` between words is still
  a blank, and every FR3 case still holds because line starts no longer
  depend on it.

### FR5: continuation-joined close lines for unquoted delimiters

- Quoted delimiter (the word holds a quote or a backslash): unchanged; a
  close line is a physical line equal to the delimiter value, under `<<-`
  once its leading tabs are removed.
- Unquoted delimiter: a candidate close line is the body's first line, or a
  later line whose preceding line does not end with an odd number of
  backslashes. A candidate is compared after joining it with the lines it
  continues into (each backslash-newline removed) and, under `<<-`, after
  removing the leading tabs of the joined line. The close extent runs from
  the candidate's start to the end of the last joined physical line, and
  the next body (if any) starts on the line after that.
- The body's first line is always a candidate, even when the line before it
  (the previous body's close line, or the operator's line) ends with a
  backslash: bash starts reading each body afresh.
- Work: building the index stays one pass over the lines per kind; checking
  a body's first line costs at most work proportional to the delimiter
  value's length, however many continuation lines follow it (NFR1).
- The existing call shape of the close-line index (one argument, the `<<-`
  flag) keeps its present meaning (physical lines); the unquoted index is
  an optional addition (IMPLEMENTATION.md internals row).

### Readers of the line index

The body extents, the position map of here-document stripping, the
here-document body extraction of the structure scan and the statement
layer's here-document records read the offsets the lexer reports. Check
each reader for an assumption that a close line is one physical line or
that line starts come from the old splitting, and keep its output
consistent with the new extents. Stage agreement over every case-table
command keeps them aligned.

Planner-found forms beyond SPEC.md (bash confirmation convention: in the
case table, tags `FR5.6` onward, only when confirmed on bash 5.3; pinned in
the agreement test in every case):

- `cat <<END\nfoo\nEN\\\nD\nrm -rf /home/sakura/valuable\nEND` — bash joins
  `EN` and `D` into a close line and runs the rm line.
- `cat <<-END\nfoo\n\tEN\\\nD\nrm -rf /home/sakura/valuable\nEND` — the same
  under `<<-`.
- `cat <<'A\\' <<B\nA\\\nB\nrm -rf /home/sakura/valuable\nB` — the second
  body's first line `B` closes it although the line before ends with a
  backslash. Deny today; it guards the first-line rule against a
  regression, so it may go in the case table without confirmation.
