# Task Plan: task0003 — Fake heredoc operators in quotes or comments capture no body

## Goal

A `<<WORD` inside quotes or a comment is not a heredoc operator. A real
operator inside a command substitution still is. Lines after a fake
operator stay where they are and are read by their actual syntax, as quote
continuation or as commands. Quote and comment state carries across lines,
and real heredoc bodies are left out of that tracking. This closes
18b99bd71e74c8dc.

## Requirements

## Design

### Operator context

- A `<<` or `<<-` is a heredoc operator only where the shell would parse it
  as one:
  - outside single quotes, double quotes and comments; or
  - inside a command substitution, even when that substitution sits inside
    double quotes (example: `"$(cat <<'EOF' … EOF)"`).
- Inside single quotes every `<<` is literal, including one within a `$(`.
- A fake operator records no heredoc. It does not appear in the recognition
  result (IMPLEMENTATION.md C3) and captures no body.

### Cross-line state

- The quote and comment state carries across line ends. A quote opened on
  one line continues on the following lines until it closes. A comment ends
  at its line end.
- The lines of a real heredoc body, from the line after the operator's line
  through the terminator line, are skipped by this tracking. Characters in
  them never open or close a quote and never start a comment for the
  command text.
- Delimiter quoting (`<<'EOF'`, `<<"EOF"`) is part of the operator, not a
  quote start (C2).
- Escaped quotes follow C2.
- The lines after a fake operator stay in place. The scan reads them with
  the carried state: inside an open quote they continue the quote,
  otherwise they are commands.

### Behavior kept

- A second operator inside a comment still captures nothing (existing case
  455).
- Real operators inside substitutions are still heredocs (existing cases
  428, 434, 435, 456, 457).

### Cost

- The state is advanced in one forward pass over the lines. Nothing
  restarts from an earlier line (NFR3).
