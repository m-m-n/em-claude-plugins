# Task Plan: task0002 — Read zsh's brace-form case statement in the substitution reader

## Goal

In both copies of `codex-hook-interactive-guard.py`, `_skip_substitution` reads `case x { ... }` to its close, so the interactive launch after such a substitution is denied. The close is one of three: a `}` at a pattern position, an `esac` at a pattern position, or a `}` at a command position of an item body with no `{ }` group open. Brace forms whose close is not certain, and heredocs inside any case statement, stay undecidable. The docstring and comments describe the reading.

## Design

### State per case statement

Each open case statement holds two values of constant size:

- its form: the `in` form or the brace form;
- for the brace form, the depth: the number of `{ }` groups open in the item body.

Both are created when the statement's stack entry is pushed at its `case` and dropped when the entry is popped at its close. A nested case statement therefore has its own values and never mixes with the enclosing statement's. The existing phase characters and the shape of the per-entry saved state keep their meaning. The item body of a statement is the statement's own entry in phase 8, so the depth belongs to the list that body reads.

### Opening (FR2)

In phase 3, after the existing handling of blanks, continuation pairs and `#`, a standalone `{` is accepted in place of `in`. The `{` is decided on the characters with the continuation pairs left out, and it must be followed by a space, a tab, a newline or the end of input. The statement becomes brace form with depth 0 and goes to phase 4.

Anything else that is not `in` stays "case without in" (undecidable), as now: a quoted `{`, an escaped `{`, and a `{` that is not a standalone word. The `in` form is read as now.

### Closing (FR3)

- **(a) `}` at a pattern position.** In phase 4 of a brace-form statement, a standalone, unquoted, unescaped `}` closes the statement exactly as a pattern-position `esac` closes one now: the entry is popped, the `[[` state is restored, the open-case count decreases, and the list that holds the statement is at a command position. The `}` is decided like a pattern-position `esac`: continuation pairs left out, followed by a blank, a newline, `;`, `&`, `|`, `)`, `<`, `>` or the end of input. In the `in` form a `}` at a pattern position starts a pattern, as now (SPEC.md A7).
- **(b) `esac` at a pattern position.** It closes a brace-form statement too, through the existing pattern-position `esac` reading, unchanged.
- **(c) `}` at a command position of the item body.** In phase 8 of a brace-form statement, a standalone `}` read at a command position of the body list itself, which the existing command-word decision already reports as a closing word:
  - at depth 0, closes the statement as in (a);
  - at depth 1 or more, lowers the depth by one and stays the compound-command closing it is now.
- A quoted or escaped `}` or `esac` starts a pattern at a pattern position and is an ordinary word in a body. A `}` at an argument position is an ordinary word.

### Depth (FR4)

- In phase 8 of a brace-form statement, a standalone `{` read at a command position of the body list itself raises the depth by one. Its existing reading as a reserved word is otherwise unchanged.
- Braces inside quotes, escaped braces, and braces inside `${ }`, substitutions, arithmetic, word parentheses, subshells or nested case statements never reach this list, so they are never counted.
- These forms are undecidable:
  - an item terminator (`;;`, `;&`, `;|`, `;;&`) read while the depth is 1 or more;
  - an `esac` that the command-word decision reports as closing, at a command position of a brace-form item body (zsh 5.9 parse error).
- An `in`-form statement keeps no depth. Its terminators, `esac` and `}` are read as now.

### Re-read (FR5)

The arithmetic-command re-read (rule 9) also restores the form and depth values to what they were at the `((`. The values of statements opened after the `((` go together with their stack entries, and the values of statements already open at the `((` return to their values at that point. The re-read budget and its accounting do not change.

### Heredoc (FR6)

Unchanged. Any `<<` that is not `<<<`, outside arithmetic, makes the chunk undecidable while a case statement is open, the brace form included. Outside case statements rule 8 applies as now.

### Shell kind

The brace form is read for every kind of shell that holds the string (SPEC.md A5).

### Documentation (this task's part of FR7)

- **Rule 2 paragraph**: the opening by a standalone `{`, the three closings (a) to (c), the depth of `{ }` groups in an item body, the readings of quoted and escaped braces and of `esac`, and that the reading applies whatever the shell kind.
- **Rule 5 paragraph**: the `}` that closes a brace-form statement gives the list that holds it a command position, as `esac` does.
- **Rule 8 paragraph**: the heredoc sentence covers the brace form. The pinned markers stay.
- **Rule 9 paragraph**: the re-read restores the brace-form state.
- **"Raises _Undecidable" paragraph**:
  - The first form becomes "no `in`, and no standalone `{`, after the subject", without the "(zsh's brace form included)" part.
  - Add the two brace-form forms from Depth.
  - Keep "a heredoc anywhere in the chunk (item bodies included) once a case statement is open". The sentence that follows it must not form the stale phrase pinned by `test_the_docstring_does_not_say_a_heredoc_is_always_undecidable`.
- **Phase list in "Contexts on the stack"**: phase 3 waits for `in` or a standalone `{`.
- **Comments**: the waiting-phase block, phase 3, phase 4, the handling of body words and the item terminator describe the brace-form reading.
