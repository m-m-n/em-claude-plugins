# Task Plan: task0001 — Quote-remove heredoc delimiter ranges in anonymous-function arguments

## Goal

In both copies of codex-hook-interactive-guard, a `(`...`)` range read as one
word in an anonymous-function argument position, when it is the delimiter of
`<<` or `<<-`, is registered as the delimiter zsh uses (quotes and
backslashes removed by the normal path's rules), so heredoc body lines are no
longer denied as launches; an unquoted `$'` / `$"` in such a range makes the
input unknowable. Regression cases pin the behavior.

## Design

### Current behavior (from the issue)
In an anonymous-function argument position, a `(`...`)` range read as one
word is appended to the word as its raw text. When that word is the target of
`<<` / `<<-`, the raw text becomes the heredoc delimiter: `<< foo('x')`
registers `foo('x')` instead of zsh's `foo(x)`, a body line equal to the raw
text ends the body early, and the next body line is inspected as a command.

### Where
The issue's location notes point at the anonymous-function argument range
reader (`_read_argument_parens`) and the word-finishing step (`_finish_word`)
in the guard. The line numbers in the issue may have drifted; locate the code
by these responsibilities.

### When the new rule applies
All three must hold:
1. The guard is reading an anonymous-function argument position.
2. The redirection waiting for its target is `<<` or `<<-` (a heredoc
   delimiter is awaited).
3. The range is read as one word, i.e. it does not take the existing
   group-opening reading for a range that contains `;`, `&`, a newline or a
   backquote (SPEC.md as-2: the group-opening reading is kept even while a
   delimiter is awaited, and neither FR1 nor FR3 applies to it).

When any of them fails, the reading is unchanged (FR4): argument ranges that
are not heredoc delimiters, `<` and `<<<` targets, group-opening ranges, and
the `PAREN_SCAN_FACTOR` scan bound.

### Delimiter derivation (FR1, FR2, FR3)
1. Take the raw text of the range, from its `(` through the matching `)`.
2. Scan it once, left to right, tracking three quote states: outside,
   single-quoted, double-quoted.
3. Outside quotes:
   - `$` immediately followed by `'` or `"` stops the scan: the whole input
     becomes unknowable, through the guard's existing unknowable outcome (no
     output) (FR3).
   - A backslash is dropped and the next character is kept literally; an
     escaped `$` is therefore a literal `$` and never triggers the rule
     above. A backslash followed by a newline is dropped together with the
     newline.
   - `'` and `"` open their quote state and are dropped.
   - Every other character is kept, including `$` not followed by a quote,
     `$x`, `$(`...`)` and nested parentheses (SPEC.md as-3).
4. Single-quoted: every character up to the closing `'` is kept verbatim,
   backslashes included; the closing `'` is dropped. `$'` / `$"` here never
   trigger the rule.
5. Double-quoted: a backslash followed by `$`, a backquote, `"`, `\` or a
   newline is dropped and the next character kept; any other backslash is
   kept together with the next character. `$'` / `$"` here never trigger the
   rule. The closing `"` is dropped.
6. If the scan ends inside a quote, the input is unknowable (NFR4).
7. The delimiter is the part of the word before the range exactly as the
   normal path already produced it (not processed again, FR2), followed by
   the processed range text, followed by whatever the normal path reads after
   the range.
8. The delimiter is registered as before; body lines are compared as before
   (`<<-` strips leading tabs before the comparison).

The rules in steps 3 to 5 are the normal path's (FR1). Reusing the normal
path's removal on the range text satisfies them when it applies to a raw
string unchanged; otherwise a dedicated single-pass routine implements them.
Because a range containing a newline or a backquote takes the group-opening
reading, the newline and backquote cases of steps 3 and 5 do not arise on
this path in practice; they are stated for parity.

### Cost and structure (NFR1, NFR2, NFR3)
- One pass per range, proportional to the range length; the range is not
  rescanned per body line or per character.
- The scan is iterative and adds no call cycle.
- Standard library only; no file, network or child-process access.

### Module docstring (FR6)
Step 4 of the module docstring's decision procedure (the anonymous-function
argument range) gains two rules, in the docstring's existing language and
style:
- When such a range is the delimiter of `<<` / `<<-`, its text is
  quote-removed by the normal path's rules before registration, and the part
  of the word outside the range is not processed again.
- An unquoted `$'` or `$"` inside such a range makes the whole input
  unknowable.

The sentences `TestGuardDocumentation` checks stay unchanged.

### Two copies (FR5)

The existing byte-identity test is the check.

- TS-8 to TS-13 are given in the unwrapped top-level form SPEC.md allows (the
  guard reads it the same way). If one is wrapped in `zsh -c "..."` instead,
  every `$` must reach the inner text as `$` (written `\$` in the outer
  text) and literal backslashes need their own escaping; otherwise the outer
  string becomes dynamic and the case is silent for the wrong reason
  (SPEC.md TS-note).
- TS-12 discriminates FR3 from plain quote removal: plain removal would
  register `foo($x)`, end the body at the second line and deny the launch.
- TS-13 discriminates FR2: zsh reads the delimiter as `a\b(x)`; processing
  the part outside the range a second time would register `ab(x)`, leave the
  launch inside the body and come out silent. If the guard's existing reading
  of the part outside the range does not carry this form to the range path
  (the case is silent for a reason unrelated to FR2), choose another
  outside-part form that a second removal would change and that the existing
  reader handles, and keep the deny expectation derived from zsh's reading.
- Labels are unique and name the TS ID.
